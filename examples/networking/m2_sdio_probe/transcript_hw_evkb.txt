What is PROVEN about the host side
----------------------------------

Controller and pads match NXP's own board support for this EVK
(mcuxsdk examples/_boards/evkbmimxrt1170):

  BOARD_SDMMC_SDIO_HOST_BASEADDR = USDHC1            <- same controller
  SDIO_RST = GPIO_AD_16 (ball N17) as GPIO9_IO15     <- same pad
  WL_RST   = GPIO_AD_31 (ball J17) as GPIO9_IO30     <- same pad
  BOARD_SDMMC_SD_IO_VOLTAGE_CONTROL_TYPE = kSD_IOVoltageCtrlByHost
                                                     <- 3.3V initial is correct

`rst_pads: ... dr=1 psr=1` for both lines, with SION forced on so PSR reflects
the actual pin. Both resets are released AND CONFIRMED HIGH AT THE PIN. This is
not "we wrote a register and hoped".

`pres_state=0xFF888088`, read through the USDHC1 pads themselves:

  bit 31:24 DLSL = 0xFF  all DAT lines idle HIGH
  bit 23    CLSL = 1     CMD idles HIGH — the R373 10K pull-up works
  bit 3     SDSTB = 1    SD clock stable
  bit 7     SDOFF = 1    clock gated while idle (normal)

Ruled out: pads not muxed, NVCC_SD rail dead, anything holding the bus low,
module dragging lines down, clock divider unstable.

`int_status=0x18000` = ERR(15) | CTOE(16). The controller sends CMD5 and nothing
answers; CC never sets. (QEMU reports 0x18001 — it also sets CC, which the
silicon correctly does not.)

Hypotheses tested and REFUTED
-----------------------------

1. Missing initialisation clocks.
   begin() used delayMicroseconds(2000) where SdFat's hardware-proven
   SdioTeensy uses SYS_CTRL[INITA]. i.MX USDHC gates SD_CLK while idle, so the
   delay emitted zero clocks. FIXED — INITA now sent, confirmed to self-clear.
   Real defect. Not this defect.

2. Module held in reset (single line, GPIO3/ALT5).
   GPIO_AD_16 -> J54 pin 23 has no pull resistor on either side, so it floats
   at POR. Drove it via GPIO3/ALT5. Read-back was inconclusive because SION was
   off. Real defect. Not this defect.

3. Wrong reset instance and sequence.
   NXP drives BOTH lines via GPIO9 (ALT10), not GPIO3: SDIO_RST high, wait
   100 ms, WL_RST high, wait 100 ms. Replicated exactly, plus SION so the pads
   could be read back. Both now confirmed high at the pin. Not this defect.

4. USDHC clock root.
   SdFat's RT1176 branch — and this driver, inheriting its note — take
   SYS_PLL2_PFD2 (mux 4, div 2 -> 198 MHz) while ASSUMING the boot ROM left
   PFD2 at 396 MHz; the imxrt1176 core's startup.c never programs PLL2. NXP
   does not assume it: BOARD_USDHC1ClockConfiguration() calls CLOCK_InitSysPll2()
   and CLOCK_InitPfd(kCLOCK_PllSys2, kCLOCK_Pfd2, 24) first.
   The trap is that a wrong root is nearly invisible — SDSTB asserts either way,
   so the controller looks healthy while the card sees the wrong frequency.
   Switched to OSC_24M (mux 1, undivided), which is always running and
   boot-independent, with BASE_CLOCK matched. Kept, because it removes an
   assumption. Not this defect either.

NOTE ON REASONING: hypothesis 4 was initially dismissed on the grounds that
SDSTB asserted and INITA self-cleared. That was wrong. SDSTB proves the divider
is stable, NOT that the frequency is what the driver believes. The dismissal
cost a cycle; the note is here so the next person does not repeat it.

What is left
------------

Firmware has now replicated NXP's documented path for this board. The remaining
candidates are physical or card-specific and cannot be settled from firmware:

1. The card is not making contact, or is faulty. It is seated and screwed, but
   "screwed down" and "all 75 contacts mating" are not the same claim.
2. The 32.768 kHz sleep clock on J54 pin 50 (from Y2 via R823, both fitted,
   Y2 powered from WL_3V3). Some combo parts will not leave internal reset
   without it. Never measured.
3. Something specific to this u-blox card versus the NXP/Murata M.2 cards NXP
   validates against.

Next steps that would actually decide it:
  * A scope on J54 pin 9 (CLK) and pin 11 (CMD) during the CMD5 attempt: does
    the clock reach the connector, and does anything drive CMD?
  * A scope or counter on J54 pin 50 to confirm Y2 is oscillating.
  * Swap in a known-good M.2 card, or try this card in a known-good host.

Not suspected
-------------

The SDIO I/O voltage. Every other M.2 signal is level-shifted to 1.8V while the
six SDIO lines run straight to the MCU, which invites the theory that NVCC_SD
must be switched to 1.8V (it is selectable — U311, an NX3L1G53GT SPDT switch,
under SD1_VSELECT). But NXP configures kSD_IOVoltageCtrlByHost, which is the
UHS switch performed AFTER identification, and standard SDIO begins at 3.3V.
The default 3.3V leg is correct for CMD5.

CONCLUSION
----------

Caveat, stated honestly
-----------------------

NXP's SDK has **no M.2 configuration for the MAYA-W1**. The module list offers
`UBX-MAYA-W1-USD` (the micro-SD adapter form factor) and `UBX-JODY-W5-M2`, but
no MAYA-W1 M.2. The build therefore used `IW416-MURATA-1XK-M2` — the only IW416
M.2 option — as the closest proxy: same chipset, same form factor.

That proxy should not matter for enumeration. The enable/reset GPIOs and the
SDIO wiring are properties of the EVKB, not of the card; per-module config is
mostly TX power tables and antenna settings, which are applied long after CMD5.
But it is not a zero-risk assumption, and it is worth noting that NXP appears
not to validate a MAYA-W1 M.2 card on this EVK at all.

Three results together:

  1. m2_sdio_probe   — IW416 never answers CMD5; bus idles high; both resets

  2. NXP wifi_cli    — NXP's own reference stack, their firmware, their board

=====================================================
1.8 V SDIO TEST — no change
=====================================================

Date: 2026-08-17 (same session). microSD OUT, M.2 card in.

Prompted by a good question: NXP's SDK has a UBX-MAYA-W1-USD config — would
building that help, given J15 and J54 share the bus? It would not: the USD path
defines WIFI_BT_USE_USD_INTERFACE and drives CONTROLLER_RESET = GPIO3 pin 9
(= GPIO_AD_10, Arduino A0 on this board), leaving the M.2 card's SDIO_RST and
WL_RST undriven — the state our very first probe run already tested and failed.

But comparing the two configs turned up something both share:

    #elif defined(WIFI_IW416_BOARD_UBX_MAYA_W1_USD)
    #define SD8978
    #define SDMMCHOST_OPERATION_VOLTAGE_1V8      <-- and the same in MURATA_1XK_M2

NXP run these modules' SDIO at 1.8 V. This driver had only ever used the 3.3 V
default. The switch is real and MCU-controllable on this board:

    GPIO_AD_34 (ball J16) ALT4 = USDHC1_VSELECT
      -> R168 (FITTED) -> SD1_VSELECT -> U311.5 (NX3L1G53GT SPDT)
      -> selects VDD_1V8 (pin 6) or SENSOR_3V3 (pins 7/8) onto SD1_POSV
      -> R1977 -> NVCC_SD

and it is SAFE rather than a level mismatch, because NVCC_SD feeds both the bus
pull-ups (R373/374/375) AND the MCU's own NVCC_SD1 pad supply (ball D14, via
R375) — the whole bus moves together. (NVCC_SD2, ball G13, is a separate domain
on FLASH_VCC and is unaffected.)

Implemented as SdioHost::useIoVoltage1V8(), applied inside begin() after RSTA
clears VEND_SPEC, then VEND_SPEC[1] = VSELECT.

Result: unchanged.

    sdio_begin=cmd5-no-response rc=-6
    int_status=0x18000 r4=0x0

The card is equally silent at 1.8 V and at 3.3 V.

CAVEAT: without a meter on TP33 (which sits directly on NVCC_SD) it cannot be
proven the rail actually moved. The evidence that it did is indirect — the bus
still idles high afterwards, which needs live pull-ups on a live rail. Anyone
with a multimeter should confirm at TP33 before treating 1.8 V as ruled out.

The capability is kept but defaults OFF: J15 is the same bus and a 3.3 V-only
microSD must not meet a 1.8 V rail.

=====================================================
*** THE CARD IS ALIVE *** — BT_WAKE_HOST held low
=====================================================

Date: 2026-08-17 (same session). First positive evidence of life from the card.

J54 pin 20 (UART_WAKE# / NXP BT_WAKE_HOST) is the ONLY card->host signal on this
connector that reaches the MCU:

    J54.20 -> R811 (0R, FITTED) -> BT_WAKE_B_3V3 -> R238 (0R, FITTED)
           -> U19.N16 = GPIO_AD_27          (no level shifter; pin 20 is 3.3 V)

Pin 21 (SDIO_WAKE#) is populated but blocked at jumper J104, open by default.
Pin 22 (BT UART TX) dies at R1901, which is DNP. So pin 20 is the only channel.

The EVKB provides NO pull-up on that net (its only members are R238, R811 and
the MCU ball), and the datasheet calls the pin open-drain, "Pullup required on
platform" -- so the MCU's internal pull-up does that job.

Measurement, with the pad configured as an input:

    bt_wake(pin20): pullup_reads=0 pulldown_reads=0 pad=0x4 ever_low=1
      -> HELD LOW externally

The control is what makes this conclusive. A single low reading could just mean
the internal pull-up never took effect. Reading under pull-UP and then pull-DOWN
separates the cases:

    up=1 down=0 -> floating, nothing drives it
    up=0 down=0 -> something ACTIVELY HOLDS IT LOW   <-- what we see
    up=1 down=1 -> something actively holds it high

`pad=0x4` confirms the pad writes stick, and `gdir=0x40008000` (bits 15 and 30
only) confirms bit 26 is an input, so the MCU is not driving it.

WHY THIS MEANS THE CARD IS POWERED AND RUNNING
----------------------------------------------

Per the datasheet (Figure 8), UART_WAKE# is driven by the card's on-board
NTB0104 level shifter, whose output-enable is tied to the card's VIO through
R12. If the card's internal 1.8 V DC-DC were not running, that shifter would be
DISABLED and its output high-Z -- and our internal pull-up would read HIGH.

It reads LOW. So the card has 3.3 V, its internal DC-DC is up, its VIO rail is
up, the on-card shifter is enabled, and it is actively asserting BT_WAKE_HOST.

The only thing on that net besides the MCU is the card, so nothing else can be
responsible.

WHAT THIS OVERTURNS
-------------------

Every earlier section of this transcript concluded "the card or the socket is
dead". That conclusion is now WRONG and is retained above only as the record of
how the investigation ran. The card is neither dead nor failing to make contact:
it is powered, running, and driving a pin the host can see.

The socket is also at least partly proven: pin 20 is making contact.

WHAT IT DOES NOT EXPLAIN
------------------------

Why the WLAN side never answers CMD5. The card being alive makes that harder to
explain, not easier -- it removes the simplest available answer.

Worth noting BT_WAKE_HOST asserted low is what an NXP BT controller does when it
has something to say to the host. This card may be sitting in its boot ROM
waiting for a firmware download over UART -- which on this board it can never
receive an answer to, because R1901 is DNP.
