uacv matched-soak baseline — 2 hosts x 2 classes, 2026-08-04
=============================================================
Purpose: establish a baseline good enough to tune the RT1176 host against
macOS. Four soaks of 21-26 minutes each, same witness images (observer
b424b0e0), same collector, same judge, all with zero xscope missing marks.

Captures archived outside git (125 MB each at high speed) at
~/Development/rt1170/uacv-captures/. Witness images preserved beside them:
    uac1_16bit_witness.xe   md5 e6403eff1def13c98781aa85b2e13c4f
    uac2_witness.xe         md5 5feb5b623f0e4cb004823b1cd49af876


The table
---------
                    polls/s   device asks   matched   steady envelope   W1
  RT1176 UAC1/FS      62.5       62.5         yes         196 B      +0.238 ppm
  RT1176 UAC2/HS      62.5      1000.0        NO         1216 B      +0.114 ppm
  macOS  UAC1/FS      62.5       62.5         yes         104 B      +0.000 ppm
  macOS  UAC2/HS     1000.0     1000.0        yes         204 B      +0.000 ppm

"steady envelope" excludes a 30 s settling window; "device asks" is the
endpoint's own declaration -- bRefresh=4 (16 ms) at full speed, bInterval=4
(1 ms) at high speed.

All four are conformant. 0 FAIL anywhere.


Finding 1: the wander tracks poll rate, not speed, packet rate or servo
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three cells poll as often as the device asks and land at 104, 196 and 204 B.
The one cell that polls a sixteenth as often opens to 1216 B -- six times
wider -- and dips to 92 B, the only cell that approaches dry-out.

The obvious confound is dead. macOS runs the same 8000 packets/s high-speed
regime as our failing cell and holds 204 B, so the wander is not inherent to
high speed, not the packet rate, and not the servo design. Our own host holds
196 B at full speed with the same EMA and slew constants. The single variable
that separates 1216 B from ~200 B is whether the host polls at the rate the
device declares.

This is a controlled result across two hosts and two classes, not an
inference from one capture.


Finding 2: a second, smaller gap that poll rate does NOT explain
-----------------------------------------------------------------
At full speed both hosts poll 62.5/s -- exactly matched -- and macOS still
holds 104 B against our 196 B, with +0.000 ppm against our +0.238 ppm.

So roughly 2x remains after poll rate is equalised. That residue is servo
quality (filter horizon, slew policy, packet-size choice), and it is worth
about half what the poll-rate gap is worth. It should be attacked second, and
only with a measurement that isolates it.


Finding 3: W2 at an integer sample rate is an artefact, confirmed twice
------------------------------------------------------------------------
macOS WARNed in both UAC1 runs, at 48 kHz, sending one-frame-short packets
0.18% of the time (282 B where 288 B is nominal at 3-byte subslots; 188 B
where 192 B is nominal at 2-byte). Our host at 44.1 kHz PASSED, because a
fractional rate gives two legal sizes and there is somewhere to vary INTO.

At an integer rate the fractional-sample law yields a single legal size while
an asynchronous host must still vary to track feedback, so every conforming
async host trips W2 there. Reproduced across two different subslot formats and
two different witness builds. The warning is honest but unavoidable; it is not
a finding about macOS.


Finding 4: the -3.39 ppm reported earlier was a short-window artefact
----------------------------------------------------------------------
A 58 s UAC1 capture had put our host at -3.39 ppm and it was recorded as
provisional. Over 21 minutes it is +0.238 ppm. Measuring before tuning was the
right call: the servo it would have sent us to "fix" was never broken.


What this licenses
-------------------
1. Raise the UAC2 feedback poll rate. FB_SLOTS is 2, sixteen frames apart.
   ITD_POOL_SIZE is 40 and the OUT ring uses 32, so the ceiling without
   growing the pool is 8 slots = 250 polls/s -- 4x, not the 16x needed to
   match macOS. Whether 250/s closes a 6x envelope gap is the next
   measurement, and it is cheap.

2. Move the EMA alpha with the poll rate. At 62.5/s and alpha 1/8 the horizon
   is ~128 ms. Quadrupling the rate at the same alpha quarters the horizon and
   walks back toward the dither-chasing bug that cost +4.8 ppm and days of
   bench time. Holding the horizon while averaging 4x more reports is the
   change that should reduce noise on the estimate.

3. Do NOT touch the UAC1 servo yet. It is +0.238 ppm over 21 minutes and its
   envelope is already inside 200 B.

4. Finish cooperative mode before tightening any loop. R7 is the only check
   that would notice a change that starts losing samples, and it has never run
   against any host.

Method note for whoever repeats this: judge from a manifest read off the
capture, not off the config's description. The macOS UAC1 soak negotiated
2-byte subslots against the 16-bit witness where the earlier run negotiated
3-byte against the 24-bit one; assuming either would have produced a wrong
frame size and an INVALID report -- which is how the guard is supposed to
behave, but it costs a soak.
