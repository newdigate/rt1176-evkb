The verification corpus -- grading the validator against hosts whose
defects were measured before it existed
======================================================================
2026-08-05. Plan 3. Reports and case definitions live in
tools/uacvalidate/corpus/; captures are archived outside the repo with
sha256 in corpus/captures/CHECKSUMS.

READ THIS FIRST: R1 IS A WARN, NOT A FAIL
------------------------------------------
Two of the cases below are hosts that never read the device's feedback
endpoint -- the founding defect of this whole investigation. The validator
reports them as WARN, and that is deliberate. USB 2.0 section 5.12.4.2 says
an asynchronous sink MUST PROVIDE feedback and that this ALLOWS the host to
adjust; every must/shall in 5.12.4 involving the host puts the obligation on
the DEVICE, and the one host-facing "must" concerns an adaptive SOURCE, the
other direction. A host that ignores an async sink's feedback endpoint is
therefore CONFORMANT. An early draft of these rules asserted it as a FAIL
citing that exact clause, from memory; reading the clause reversed it. The
tool says WARN and shows the arithmetic -- a block correction every 8 s at
+85 ppm -- rather than overclaiming a violation it cannot support.

     8414928 non-silent frames. The injection is the exact inverse of

  E  090eadb~1   device-swap wedge                           OK
     Host behaviour reproduced exactly: through 140 s of phase 2 the console
     read audio=ready alt=1 pkts/s=0 with the FIFO frozen at 3854 -- claim

     recorded audio=ready uac2=0 alt=1 pkts/s=0.
     The tool reports capture-level INVALID rather than the per-rule SKIPs
     the plan predicted, and the reason is a FINDING: uacvEmitBlock sits
     inside the decoupler's packet-arrival path, so a host that transmits
     nothing yields a capture with zero state blocks -- indistinguishable
     from a dead collector. INVALID is the honest answer to that input, but
     it means the validator can only decline to rule here, never attribute.
     The expectation was corrected to what the tool does AND strengthened:
     expect_no_pass asserts that no rule may PASS on a capture where nothing
     was transmitted, whatever shape the tool uses to say so.

The negative controls matter as much as the positives: every rule that
fired against a defective host comes back clean at master, so the corpus
proves the tool can say PASS as well as FAIL.

INJECTED DEFECTS (rules with no reachable historical negative)
---------------------------------------------------------------
  D2 r3-right-justify.patch                                  OK
     R3 FAIL, or_acc=0x0000FFFF. See above.

  H  r4b-partial-frame.patch                                 OK
     One extra byte per microframe. R4b FAIL on all 1044500 packets, sizes
     161 and 193 B in the histogram. R4a stayed PASS -- 193 B is far inside
     the declared 800 -- which is why oversize and misalignment are two
     rules and not one. W2 independently WARNed that the sizes are off the
     fractional-sample pair, a second signal from a rule that knows nothing
     about frame alignment.

  F  w3-no-ema.patch                                         OK
     R1 PASS with W3 WARN at +10.03 ppm: the host polling 38166 times and
     drifting anyway. The report names the mechanism -- "a servo that chases
     the raw dithered report rather than its mean produces exactly this
     signature" -- which is the pairing W3 exists for and which R1 alone
     cannot see.

     ATTEMPT 1 DID NOT REPRODUCE, and that is a finding. Removing the EMA
     alone, leaving the shipping 1000 polls/s reader, gave +0.389 ppm over
     600 s: seven times the shipping servo's -0.052 ppm, but inside the
     1 ppm floor, so W3 correctly PASSed. At one poll per frame the slew
     limiter (4 mHz/frame) is itself the low-pass filter -- each raw report
     is superseded before sizing can travel toward a rail. The historical
     +4.8 ppm was measured at 62.5 polls/s, where a report persists 16
     frames and the asymmetry becomes bias. So the INJECTION was wrong, not
     the expectation: the patch now reproduces the historical configuration
     rather than the threshold being moved. The failed attempt is kept in
     cases.json.

     Worth carrying forward: the EMA's protective value shrinks as the poll
     rate approaches the device's report rate. That is NOT a licence to drop
     it -- the horizon invariant still guards the 62.5 polls/s FS reader,
     which has no such margin.

  G  r4a-oversize.patch                                      OK
     R4a FAIL: 1308 packets of 832 B against an advertised 800. R4b stayed
     PASS -- 832 is 26 whole 32 B frames -- so G and H prove the two size
     rules distinct in both directions.

     TWO PREDICTIONS FALSIFIED getting here, both mine. (a) R4a was called
     structurally blocked, on the theory that a packet beyond wMaxPacketSize
     is a babble condition XUD would refuse before the decoupler could count
     it. False: the first attempt put 832 straight into the device's size
     histogram. The device stack accepts and counts it. (b) That attempt
     still never reached R4a, because the manifest-contradiction guard fired
     first -- mean packet 340.3 B against a 176.4 B prediction, 1.93x -- and
     a mis-declared format is indistinguishable from an oversizing host in
     the mean. The guard is right; the consequence is that R4a can only rule
     on oversize RARE or SMALL enough to keep the mean within one audio
     frame of prediction. The INJECTION was retuned (1 frame in 100, and
     rate-neutral), not the guard.

     Worth knowing: two of the judge's safety mechanisms can pre-empt each
     other. A host whose defect is gross enough to move the mean packet size
     gets "cannot judge" rather than a cited FAIL naming the defect. Honest,
     but blunt -- and only fixable with a way to tell the two causes apart,
     which the capture alone does not carry.

     UNREQUESTED FINDING: R3 also FAILed on this capture,
     or_acc=0xFFFF00E0. The low byte carrying data means the oversize packet
     knocked the device's subslot unpacking out of alignment -- its OUT
     geometry is sized for wMaxPacketSize. Collateral damage caught by a
     rule that knows nothing about packet size, and evidence the defect is
     materially harmful rather than merely nonconformant.

WHAT THE CORPUS CAUGHT THAT WAS NOT THE VALIDATOR
--------------------------------------------------
1. SETTLE-WINDOW CONTAMINATION (fixed). Every other script on this bench
   starts the collector, waits, then flashes -- safe only because the
   resident firmware IS the thing under test. Here it is the previous
   case's host. Case B's first run recorded 36577 feedback polls from a
   host with no feedback reader compiled in: 36577 / 1000 polls/s = 36.6 s,
   exactly the settle window, during which master's build was still
   running. W1 then fitted that host's flat closed-loop stretch and
   reported +0.095 ppm for an open-loop host -- a plausible, quotable,
   wrong number. Both runners now flash BEFORE the collector exists.

2. CONSOLE-INDUCED XSCOPE DROPS (fixed). Holding the VCOM for a whole
   300 s capture cost one missing mark, which correctly invalidates the
   report since every counter becomes a lower bound. The console reader is
   now bounded to 30 s.

3. AN UNVERIFIABLE CHANGE, WITHDRAWN. 3e66161 had applied the HS ring's
   wire-order priming to the FS ring "by the same reasoning", with no way
   to verify it (R7 needs a cooperative witness; the UAC1 witness cannot
   carry a 24-bit pattern in a 2-byte subslot). It is now reverted --
   USBHost_t36 bef7c9f -- because an unverifiable change to a working path
   is not worth carrying. FS keeps its 32 ms start-of-stream seam, which is
   inaudible and which nothing on this bench can currently see.

   Honesty note: the revert was first committed claiming that change had
   BROKEN FS on silicon. Re-testing disproved it -- the symptom persisted
   after the revert, and the repo's own UAC1 firmware streams fine at
   master on the same witness (pkts/s=999, feedback healthy). The message
   was corrected. There is no FS driver regression.

OPEN
----
* The corpus host streams FS at every historical corpus commit and HS at
  master, but on FS AT MASTER it claims, returns true from beginStreaming,
  and transmits nothing. The shipping firmware does not, so this is a
  harness question, not a driver one. The clean-FS control therefore uses
  usb_audio_graph_test -- arguably the better control anyway. Unexplained;
  recorded in cases.json rather than papered over.
* Case E's blind spot: emit the state block on a timer independent of
  packet arrival, so "device up, host silent" becomes a positive
  observation. That changes the wire format's emission rule, not its
  layout.
* Nothing outstanding on rule coverage: every rule has now fired on
  hardware. What remains open is the corpus-host FS anomaly above and case
  E's instrument blind spot.
