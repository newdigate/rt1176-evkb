CM7 CPU BUDGET FOR USB HOST AUDIO -- EVKB, 2026-08-06
=====================================================

Image: usb_audio_duplex_test, DUPLEX_ECHO=ON CPU_BUDGET=ON, 44100 Hz,
device IN 1ch/16 taken as 1ch, OUT 2ch/16. UAC1 dongle on J47 (OTG2),
full-speed, both directions streaming simultaneously (echo/monitor path).

--- 1. THE NAIVE METRIC IS A TRAP -------------------------------------
This sketch polls in a free-running loop, so it is ~100% busy by
construction and its 'idle' is only the share of spin that missed the
instrumented calls. At its natural 145127 loops/s the USB service path
MEASURES as 69.1% of the CM7. That number is nearly all waste.

--- 2. SEPARATING WORK FROM SPIN --------------------------------------
The steal staircase varied the loop rate over a 19x range (145127 ->
7528 loops/s) while the device kept streaming. Regressing each bucket
against loop rate splits per-call spin (slope) from the work that must
happen however often you poll (intercept):

  bucket   %/Mloop   intercept%      R^2
     svc   424.320        0.888  1.00000
    task    20.955       -0.006  0.99999
    echo    23.179        0.281  0.99995
   TOTAL   468.454        1.163  1.00000

  => GENUINE USB DUPLEX WORK = 1.16% of the CM7 at 996 MHz.
  task's intercept is ~0: myusb.Task() does nothing per call once
  enumeration is done. R^2=1.00000 over 15 points.

--- 3. isr/s = 0, ALWAYS ----------------------------------------------
The EHCI interrupt never fired once, at any steal level. USBHS_USBINTR
is 0 (ehci.cpp:287) and the iso rings are armed ioc=false, so the whole
USB host audio path is POLLED. There is no interrupt cost to move.

--- 4. THE REAL CONSTRAINT IS LATENCY, NOT THROUGHPUT -----------------
The thief burns pct% of every millisecond as ONE contiguous block, so
pct maps directly to a stall length: 60% = 600us, 85% = 850us.

  85% (850us stalls), 5 min soak  -- FAILS.
    OUT fifo drains monotonically 2830 -> 1494 and keeps going;
    under 189 -> 1387, and the RATE ACCELERATES (0.36/s in the first
    half, 7.73/s in the second); derived falls to 43681 Hz.
    Frozen and clean for the first ~80 s, which is exactly why a short
    observation overestimates headroom.

  60% (600us stalls), 4.3 min soak -- HEALTHY.
    OUT fifo oscillates 3092-4094 with NO downward trend;
    derived locked at 44100 Hz; under frozen at 155 for 180 s
    (up=54 -> 234) with ~0.4/s residual over the whole span.

  => Safe contiguous stall is bracketed: 600us OK, 850us not.
     The knee was NOT pinned down; no level between the two was soaked.

--- 5. WHAT THE 10-SECOND STAIRCASE CLAIMED, AND WHY IT WAS WRONG -----
With a well-primed FIFO the ramp ran clean all the way to 88.56% taken
and only cracked at the end of that step. The 5-minute soak at a LOWER
level (85%) then failed outright. Short steps measure the buffer, not
the steady state.

--- 6. CAVEATS --------------------------------------------------------
  - rpt=1.58% of every window is this sketch's own 250-byte heartbeat
    at 115200 baud. It is bucketed separately, not hidden in idle, but
    a real application would not pay it.
  - Full-speed device (1000 frames/s). A high-speed device runs 8000
    microframes/s and was not measured here.
  - Starting up UNDER load is harder than running under it: the fixed
    soaks enumerated with the thief already active and began at
    fifo=1148, versus the ramp which primed at 0% first.
