What this proves
----------------

The uSDHC1 path is good on silicon, end to end, at a physical connector on the
SAME NETS that reach the M.2 socket:

  * USDHC1 controller, its clocking and its command engine
  * the GPIO_SD_B1_00..05 pads and their mux
  * the pull-ups on CMD and DATA
  * the bus as far as a connector, at real data rates, with CRC intact

It also settles a question left open in the M.2 investigation: SdFat's RT1176
branch drives the USDHC1 root from SYS_PLL2_PFD2 while ASSUMING the boot ROM
left PFD2 at ~396 MHz. This run passes with that assumption in force, so on this
board the boot ROM DOES leave PFD2 up. M2Radio's SdioHost was switched to
OSC_24M to remove the assumption; that remains a reasonable robustness choice,
but the PLL2 path is confirmed available and should be restored when throughput
matters — 24 MHz is ample for enumeration and firmware download, not for
sustained Wi-Fi traffic.

Bearing on the M.2 result
-------------------------

Combined with:
  * m2_sdio_probe — the IW416 never answers CMD5, bus idling high, and
  * NXP's own wifi_cli — hangs at "Initialize WLAN Driver" on the same board,

this control removes the host from suspicion entirely. The uSDHC1 bus works.
The fault is the M.2 card or the M.2 socket.
