★★ WHAT SILICON PROVED THAT QEMU COULD NOT
------------------------------------------

1. THE COORDINATE→WIDGET MAPPING IS CORRECT THROUGH LVGL'S OWN HIT-TESTING.
   Five buttons checked in a fixed order a mis-mapped axis makes impossible —
   the same argument as v2, but this time the hit-test and click delivery are
   LVGL's, not the example's.

2. THE DRAG IS A REAL FINGER: DRAG_END x=610 moves=25, against the model's
   exact x=598 moves=9. Twenty-five distinct handle positions is a human
   stroke sampled at ~100 Hz; the scripted run cannot produce it, and a
   binding that forwarded Idle as released could not have produced ANY of it
   (the handle would have died at the left edge — measured in QEMU negative
   test 1, transcript_qemu commit 00e8fcf).

3. THE PRIMARY-CONTACT POLICY HELD UNDER A REAL HAND: two simultaneous
   fingers, the primary lifted first, and TRAP stayed UNCHECKED while HOLD
   clicked clean. The QEMU phase-3b instants exercise this deterministically;
   a hand's imprecise, non-simultaneous lifts exercise it the way users will.

4. THE HARDWARE SCENE CHECKSUM EQUALS THE QEMU GOLDEN (0xE1559496): the
   pre-touch render is byte-deterministic across emulator and silicon.

5. REAL-WORLD POLL TRAFFIC: IDLE_POLLS=23076 against BUFFERS=312 — idle
   outnumbers fresh data ~74:1 on silicon, which is the ratio the Idle latch
   exists for. POLL_FAILS=0: the 400 kHz bus stayed clean throughout.

TEARING (spec §8.1 accepted it; recording what was actually seen): operator
reports the handle "tracked cleanly, no tearing noticed, some very minor
flickering". Consistent with the accepted direct-render trade — a transient
artefact on a moving widget, no sliced frames observed. v4's double-buffer
milestone remains the fix.


Bench finding worth keeping: THE FIRST WATCH'S WINDOW ARMS AT RESET
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Two earlier attended attempts failed with the identical signature
(BTN1_FAIL reason=timeout, IDLE_POLLS=6047, BUFFERS=3): the 60 s window for
button 1 expired before the operator reached the bench, the phase chain
short-circuited, and the operator's subsequent taps played to watches that
had already exited — while the widgets still visibly reacted, because loop()
keeps LVGL live. On a remotely-driven bench that failure LOOKS like success
on the glass. Fixed in 6cbab5e: button 1 waits 10 minutes (the operator-
arrival window); the rest keep 60 s. QEMU is indifferent — its scripted
first tap arrives within milliseconds.

Also observed on those runs: BUFFERS=3 with no finger on the glass, twice,
identically — the real GT911 publishes a few buffers after reset with no
contact. Harmless here (they carry zero contacts and the binding latches),
but worth knowing before anyone asserts BUFFERS==0 on an untouched panel.

  pkill LinkServer; pkill redlinkserv; pkill crt_emu_cm_redlink
  cd examples/display/lvgl_rk055_touch_test

  /Applications/LinkServer_26.6.137/LinkServer flash MIMXRT1176:MIMXRT1170-EVKB verify build/lvgl_rk055_touch_test.elf
  python3 ../../../tools/rt1170-console.py /dev/cu.usbmodem<probe> 115200 &
  /Applications/LinkServer_26.6.137/LinkServer run MIMXRT1176:MIMXRT1170-EVKB build/lvgl_rk055_touch_test.elf &
  # then follow the TOUCH_PROMPT lines; button 1 waits 10 minutes for you.

  Do NOT hold the VCOM during load or verify (DAP status 131). There is no
  standalone `LinkServer reset` in 26.6.137; a backgrounded `run` is the reset.
