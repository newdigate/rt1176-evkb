★★ ANALYSIS — the raw-op facts
------------------------------
1. FILLS: THE CPU WINS OR TIES ALMOST EVERYWHERE.  A fill is write-only, and
   the CM7's store pipeline streams ~630 MB/s into non-cacheable SDRAM at
   565 (2906 us full-screen) and ~525 MB/s at 32 bpp (7027 us) -- the v6
   ~20 MB/s figure was for READ+WRITE copies, not writes.  The PXP fill runs
   ~310/500 MB/s.  One anomaly both depths: the 240-px-wide row (fill_240x160)
   is CPU-slow (480/1152 us) and PXP-won -- a row-width effect worth knowing,
   not a general PXP fill win.
2. BLITS: PXP wins 6.0-10.5x at 565 (min blit_240x160 4893/813, max
   blit_32x32 241/23) and 8.9-17.2x at 32 bpp (min blit_240x160 11493/1292,
   max blit_120x40 1578/92).  Ranges recomputed from the table rows above --
   final-review correction (the earlier 7.5x/12x floors skipped the
   240-px-wide rows).
3. COMPOSITES (565): PXP wins 28.6-49.2x (max = comp_400x300, 114532/2330).
   CORRECTION (final review): the previously stated "63x" was comp_720x1280's
   CPU divided by the BLIT_720x1280 PXP row (879963/14093) -- a row mix-up;
   the true full-screen composite ratio is 879963/18149 = 48.5x.  The
   full-screen SW alpha blend costs 880 ms; the PXP does it in 18.1 ms.
   ENGINE_DELTA=1 LSB on every case (the two engines' rounding differs by
   design; each path asserted vs its own oracle).
4. Bare-op overhead: ~2-3 us program+enable+wait -- negligible against any
   task the evaluate gate would accept.
5. 32-bpp X-byte: the engines differ (LVGL writes 0xFF, PXP writes 0) --
   fills/blits routed to PXP at 32 bpp WOULD move framebuffer goldens (this
   corrects spec §6's "goldens unchanged" assumption; carried to the stop).

★★ THE PROJECTION (v9 P2 — the stop's decision input)
-----------------------------------------------------
Census (hardware, both scenes at 32 bpp): the ONLY accelerable task type
present is FILL (flip: 120@38400-bucket + 122@full-frame; touch: 208 + 128).
BORDER, BOX_SHADOW and LABEL have no PXP op.  NEITHER SCENE GENERATES A
SINGLE IMAGE TASK — the ops where the PXP is spectacular (blits 8.9-17.2x
at 32 bpp, composites 28.6-49.2x) never occur in the current workloads.

Fill economics at 32 bpp: CPU wins or ties every measured size (full-frame:
CPU faster by ~306 us; mid-bucket: a wash) except the 240-px-row anomaly.
PRICING ASSUMPTION -- RESOLVED BY MEASUREMENT (2026-08-01 addendum, two
consecutive silicon runs, values identical):
    DRAW n=fill_200x160 CPU_us=484 PXP_us=510 OK   (the touch widgets' geometry)
    DRAW n=fill_120x120 CPU_us=111 PXP_us=118 OK   (the flip box's geometry)
Both census geometries are CPU-WON at 32 bpp: the width-120 extrapolation was
correct and fill_240x160 is an isolated row-width anomaly.  The projection's
"~0" mid-bucket pricing was, if anything, generous to the PXP; the sign does
NOT flip and THE DECLINE STANDS ON MEASURED IN-BUCKET ROWS.  The same runs
also recovered the two 32x32 timing rows lost to capture gaps earlier:
    DRAW n=fill_32x32 CPU_us=11-16 PXP_us=16 OK
    DRAW n=blit_32x32 CPU_us=482 PXP_us=35 OK
(DRAWS=15, DRAW_BENCH_OK, both runs; 565-build addendum rows measured in QEMU
only -- the projection's depth is 32 bpp and the 565 verdicts are unchanged.)  The hardware numbers for the two
new rows land with the next silicon run.

Projected per 120-frame run, routing every fill to the PXP:
  flip:  122 x (-306 us) + 120 x (~0) - 362 tasks x 1.03 us eval  ~= -38 ms
  touch: 128 x (-306 us) + 208 x (~0) - 661 x 1.03 us             ~= -40 ms
i.e. ADOPTION WOULD MAKE BOTH CURRENT SCENES ~0.3 ms/frame SLOWER.

Caveats (spec §5, restated): the scenes are simple and fill-dominated by
construction; a census cannot see tasks LVGL merges or skips; ladder sizes
bracket real task sizes.  None of these caveats changes the sign: there is
nothing for the PXP to win at in these scenes today.

RECOMMENDATION: DECLINE adoption now (the spec's legitimate "not worth it"
outcome).  The bench, census probe and tables stand as the instrument; the
moment an image-heavy scene exists (icons, sprites, photos — where 8.9-49x
awaits), re-run the census and revisit.  The 32-bpp X-byte golden implication
(engines differ: 0xFF vs 0) is on record for that future decision.
